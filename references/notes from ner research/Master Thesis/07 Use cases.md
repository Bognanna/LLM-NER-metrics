In this chapter I present how the CDEF metric performes on particular use cases with comparison to other metrics. The comparison is made between following metrics:
- CDE
- EF
- CDEF-0.5
- CDEF-0.1
- precision
- recall
- F1-score
- F1-score with soft matching strategy and threshold = 0.5

For each use case I generated two outputs. One was generated by model llama3.1 instructed with 0-shot prompt, the second one by model llama3.1 instructed with Few-shot prompt. According to athours, the responses from the 1-shot prompting should be better.

Embeddings are taken from nomic-embed-text model .

```

GOLD_ENTITIES: [['pain in the lower back', 'Symptom'], ['pain in the right leg', 'Symptom']]
```

Empirically, 1-shot entities are closer to gold entities than 0-shot enitites. There are too many 0-shot enitites. Morover, only the  \[`pain`, `Symptom`] entity captures the meritum of the gold entites, that are descriptions of pain in two different body parts. 1-shot entities are in the same number as gold entities. Entity \[`pain in the lower back`, `Symptom`\] is the same as gold entity, enitty \[`occasionally in the right leg pain`, `Symptom`\] is very similar to \[`pain in the right leg`, `Symptom`\] the difference is the reordered words and one addidtional word, describing the frequency of the symptom. 

CDE captures the closer distance of 1-shot entities to gold entities. EF for 0-shot entities is greater than 0, which correctly indicates, that there are to many generated entities. For 1-shot enitites EF is equal 0, that means, there are exact number of generated entities as gold enitites. CDEF as the combination of these 2 measures, shows better quality of 1-shot entities than 0-shot enitites.

Precission, recall and F1-score also presents that 1-shot entities are closer to ground truth than 0-shot entities - this is beacuse among 1-shot entities there is one exact the same as gold enitites. CDEF metric main advantage is to accept entites that are not semantically the same, but has very close meaning. Precission, Recall and F1 accept entities that are exact the same, which is not sufficient for LLM generated output (e.g. US1.2. `['shortness of breath', 'Symptom']` vs. gold `['shortness of breath on exertion', 'Symptom']`). F1 with soft matchnig partially solves this problem, because it looks for the longest substring inside gold entity, that reflects the generated entity. However, when an additional word is added to the entity (generated `['feeling like stomach is full and empty at the same time', 'Symptom']` vs. gold `['feeling stomach is full and empty at the same time', 'Symptom']`) or the words in an enity are reordered (generated `['occasionally in the right leg pain', 'Symptom']` vs. `['pain in the right leg', 'Symptom']`) it also fails. Because CDEF base on similarity of embeddings it captures all of this situations and still this entities accounts as very similar.
